# Code review and better search (November 2026)

In November the whole codebase was reviewed again: the studio, the website,
the capture app, the shared packages, the image worker, the FileMaker import
and the database. Then search across the studio was rebuilt on OpenSearch.

- **Part 1** lists what was wrong and is now fixed. It needs no decision.
- **Part 2** is the new search: how it works, and what it needs on the server.
- **Part 3** lists the choices the fixes left to you. Each has a recommended
  answer.
- **Part 4** is what you, or someone with the server password, have to do, in
  order.
- **Part 5** is how all of this was checked.

**How to answer Part 3:** reply "all as recommended", or give the numbers you
want otherwise (for example "3: cap it; 9: allow both registers"). Nothing in
Part 3 changes until you answer.

## Part 1: what was wrong, now fixed

The review found 222 suspected defects. A second, independent reviewer
confirmed 220 of them, and all 220 were fixed. A few are only partly fixed
because the rest needs your answer in Part 3. Each fix was then reviewed again
by a third reviewer. The ones that matter day to day:

### Saving records

- **Publications and Past exhibitions never saved.** On a work's editor you
  could type a line, see "All changes saved", and lose it. They now save.
- **The accountant's edits were silently dropped.** The database lets the
  accountant read the figures but not change them, yet the editor said
  "Saved". The editor now opens only for admin and staff. Everywhere else a
  refused save says so in plain words and is not retried in a loop.
- **An editor left open could undo newer work.** Coming Back to an editor
  showed the old record, and the next keystroke saved that old form over
  newer edits. A contact left open could also overwrite consent or
  do-not-mail changes the website had made in the meantime. Both are
  guarded now. The record editor now sends only the fields you actually
  changed, so a stale tab can no longer change status, location or the
  website switch behind your back.
- **Currencies.** A purchase in a currency other than GBP, EUR, USD, CHF or
  JPY (INR from the capture app, for example) was relabelled GBP on the next
  save, and the foreign amount became the Cost £. Every ECB currency is now
  kept and converted. Switching a purchase away from GBP no longer leaves the
  foreign figure in Cost £. Amounts typed with a decimal comma (1.250,50 or
  1250,50) are read correctly. Part 3 item 6 has a read-only check for rows
  that may already be wrong.
- **Sold £ recomputed on unrelated edits.** Saving any field of a sold
  foreign-currency work recomputed its Sold £. It now changes only when the
  price, currency or sale date changes.
- **Image uploads.** Photographs were filed under the role selected when the
  upload *finished*, not when they were dropped. An upload that took over
  three minutes stayed "Processing…" for good.

### Lists, inventory and documents

- **"Add to list" on a live list** reported success, filed nothing, and could
  clear your flags. Live lists are no longer offered as targets.
- **Paging by a sort column** could repeat or skip works between pages. A
  page number past the end failed outright.
- **A shared document was deleted for every work it was attached to**,
  either by "Remove" on one work or when one work was purged from the Trash.
  It is now unlinked from that one work only.
- **Deleted photographs, documents, shipment papers and portraits stayed in
  storage for ever.** The database now queues their files, and a weekly job
  removes them 90 days later. That is past the 30 daily backups, so restoring
  a backup never points at a missing file.
- **Deletes with no confirm step:** "Delete list" and a photograph's Delete
  acted on a single click. Both now ask first.
- **A sold work could be deleted and purged** together with its purchase and
  sale record, against the retention schedule. Staff can no longer do this,
  and the nightly purge skips sold works. Only an administrator can remove
  one for good (Part 3 item 4).

### Money, dashboard and stock book

- **Dashboard "Net profit"** was the old FileMaker figure or the margin
  before VAT, and "Total sales" counted non-J.v.d.B. and trashed works. Both
  now follow the VAT rule in the project notes.
- **A contact's consignments and co-owner shares never loaded** on their
  page. The query used a link that no longer exists.
- **Consignment shares** showed J.v.d.B. receiving the costs for a work not
  yet sold. They now show no share until there is a sale (Part 3 item 2
  covers sales below cost).
- **The stock book marked almost every row "No seller or purchase
  invoice".** It now looks for the evidence where the studio actually keeps
  it.
- **CSV exports opened garbled in Excel** (macrons, accents, Japanese). They
  now carry the mark Excel needs. The large exports read every row, however
  many there are.

### Contacts, newsletters, offers and data protection

- **The sanctions screen could never match** a contact with a two-word first
  or last name, and marked them Clear. Fixed.
- **Contact import ignored the studio's own export headers** and imported
  rows without names. A "Title" column was used as the whole greeting.
- **The subject-access export left out** the works a contact consigned or
  co-owns, and the details of their appointments. A failed read exported an
  empty section without saying so.
- **The 30-day purge of unconfirmed newsletter sign-ups** left the person's
  whole record in the activity log for six years. It could also delete a
  sign-up the gallery had since started working with. Both fixed.
- **A retention period saved from a blank field became 1 day**, and the
  nightly run would then purge that class. Fixed.
- **Data-subject request due dates** ran past the legal month at month ends
  (31 January became 3 March). Fixed.
- **Newsletters**: one whose every email was refused was marked Sent and
  locked for good. One could go out with a dead unsubscribe link. Staff
  sends left no send log. All fixed.
- **Offers**: addresses that had hard-bounced were still mailed. Price and
  note autosave said "Saved" when the write failed. "Find contacts to add"
  could not find a contact by full name.
- **Resetting your own password** from the Users list signed you out before
  the new password was shown, and deleted your passkeys.

### Calendar and appointments

- **Visits could not be marked completed or no-show** once their start time
  had passed. They now appear under "Needs an outcome".
- **Saving a calendar event** lost line breaks in its location and notes, and
  shortened an event that ended at midnight. One event with an unusual
  time-zone name broke the whole shared calendar feed.
- **The dashboard's "Next 10 days"** listed yesterday's all-day events under
  Today throughout British Summer Time.

### Website

- **Unsubscribe links acted on a plain page load**, so mail scanners
  unsubscribed readers who never clicked. The link now asks for one click.
- **The private offer page ignored the order staff had arranged.** It could
  also show a label or back shot as a work's picture, or tell a collector
  the link had expired when the database merely failed to answer. Trashed
  works now drop off it at once.
- **Website search was accent-sensitive**: "hozan" did not find Hatanaka
  Hōzan. Fixed.
- **The full-screen view** opened at the photograph's own pixel size instead
  of fitted to the screen. The archive and publication grids forgot your
  place when you came Back.
- **Bounces and spam complaints** could be lost when their write failed.
  Email opens counted as offer page views.
- **Booking** accepted times already past and dates that do not exist. Its
  invitation used a floating time, so a visitor whose calendar is not on UK
  time got the wrong hour.
- **Accessibility**: modal layers now take, keep and return keyboard focus.
  Collapsed "Read more" text is out of the tab order. When Turnstile cannot
  load, the forms explain why instead of telling visitors to wait.

### Capture app

- **Pushing twice created duplicate stock records.** This happened after a
  timeout, a reload or a second phone, and failed photo copies were dropped
  without a word. A push can now be repeated safely.
- **Business-card scanning always failed.** It sent a setting the default AI
  model rejects. The AI replies are now checked before use.
- **Invoices linked to existing inventory never reached the studio.** Fixed.
- **Deleting a contact in capture** hard-deleted it, ignoring legal holds
  and the admin-only erasure rule. It can now only undo a contact added in
  the last 48 hours, and never one with a hold.
- **Pushing created makers that were published to the website at once.**
  Capture now links only to an existing maker. Otherwise it writes the name
  into the comments for the cataloguer.
- **Business-card photos were kept for ever.** They are now deleted as soon
  as they are read. Anthropic and Google Maps, which capture uses, are now in
  the processor list and the Privacy Notice.

### Documents, image worker and FileMaker import

- **PDFs garbled macrons and Japanese**: "Yoneda Bishō" printed as "Yoneda
  BishM". Certificates, fact sheets and labels now embed Noto fonts.
- **Avery labels printed wrapped lines on top of each other.** Fixed.
- **The image worker could strand photographs at "processing" for ever.**
  Nothing reclaimed them. It now retries them after 15 minutes and gives up
  after three attempts. This takes effect when the worker is updated
  (Part 4 step 3).
- **Transparent backgrounds turned black** in the display copy. They are
  now white.
- **The FileMaker import's "finalize" step** was not safe to run twice, and
  it never applied the category and location mappings. A dry-run command
  now fills those in for works already imported (Part 3 item 25).

### Clean-up

Dead code and unused dependencies were removed: `savePiece`, old components
and unused exports. `pnpm audit` is still at zero. CI now also runs the unit
tests and the database tests.

## Part 2: the new search

### What changes for staff

- **One search box finds more.** The command palette (press `/`) now groups
  its results into Works, Makers, Contacts and Records (offers, documents,
  shipments, lists and newsletters). It ends with "All works matching …" and
  "All contacts matching …".
- **Accents and typing slips are forgiven.**
  - "yosai" finds Yokota Yōsai, and "Kramer" finds Krämer.
  - "Rokubai" (a typo) finds the right maker, shown as "Similar matches".
  - A full stock number such as 2024-0003 still finds exactly that one
    work. Numbers and email addresses are never "fuzzy".
- **It searches more of the record.** Works are found by provenance,
  exhibitions, publications, condition report, inscriptions, box notes,
  medium, period, category, maker names (Japanese, romanised and other
  names) and former stock numbers. Contacts are found by name,
  organisation, email, phone in any format (+44 20…, 020…, digits only),
  city and country. Old-form kanji find the modern form (濱田 finds 浜田).
- **The same search everywhere.** The inventory overview, the add-works
  boxes on offers, documents, shipments and lists, the contact pickers, the
  contacts page and the makers page all use it.
- **Nothing gets worse.** The old database search still runs every time,
  and OpenSearch's results are added to it. A work saved a second ago, or a
  server that is down, slow or still filling, gives at least today's
  results.
- **Who sees what is unchanged.** Every result is loaded through your own
  login, so the database's rules still decide what each role sees. Only
  admin and staff searches reach OpenSearch. The accountant and the capture
  app keep the old search. Invoices, financial figures, contact notes,
  addresses and consent records are never put in the search index.

### What it needs

OpenSearch runs on the gallery's own server, beside the database. It has its
own small private network, the server's own address only, and two passwords
behind Caddy: one that can only search, one that can only index. The plugin
that logs what people type is removed, and it uses at most 1 GB of memory.
Until you do Part 4 step 4, everything above except the extra recall and
typo tolerance already works on the database alone.

The full procedure, with checks and undo for every step, is in
`docs/ops/opensearch-runbook.md`. It takes about an hour.

## Part 3: your choices

### Money and the stock book

1. **May the accountant enter figures?** Today the accountant can read every
   figure but change none, as the database rules have always said. The
   editor now says so instead of pretending to save. *Recommended: keep it
   read-only.* If the accountant should enter purchase £ and import VAT,
   say so, and the rules and the editor will change together.
2. **A consignment sold below its costs.** Example: sold for £300 with £500
   of restoration costs. Today J.v.d.B. is reimbursed all £500 and the
   consignor gets nothing and owes the difference. The alternative is to cap
   the reimbursement at the net, so J.v.d.B. gets at most £250 after VAT.
   *Recommended: follow your consignment agreements; if they say nothing,
   keep today's rule.*
3. **Old FileMaker sales in a foreign currency.** Typing a missing sale date
   on such a sale converts it at the ECB rate and replaces FileMaker's £.
   That can change a VAT quarter already filed. *Recommended: protect those
   sales, so their £ is never reconverted and can only be typed.* This is a
   small change.
4. **Removing a sold work for good.** Staff can no longer do it, and the
   nightly purge leaves sold works in the Trash. An administrator still can,
   for a true duplicate. *Recommended: keep the administrator exception.*
5. **Trashed works in the stock book** (October item 18, still open). They
   still appear there and in its exports, with their VAT. *Recommended: run
   the appendix query in `owner-decisions-2026-10.md` with the accountant,
   then say yes to removing them.*
6. **Rows that may already have the wrong currency.** Before this fix,
   saving relabelled any currency outside the five as GBP. To list such rows
   (read-only), run `select purchase_currency, sell_currency, count(*) from
   piece_financials group by 1, 2;`. *Recommended: run it once and look at
   anything unexpected.*
7. **Offer INR and HKD in the editor.** They are kept and converted, but
   they are not in the editor's currency list. *Recommended: add them.*

### Lists and inventory

8. **May lists hold works from the non-J.v.d.B. register?** Bulk "Add to
   list" refuses them; a work's own "Add to list" files them; the database,
   exports and website handle both. *Recommended: allow both registers
   everywhere.*
9. **A live list stops at its newest 500 works.** The studio now says so.
   *Recommended: keep the limit.*

### Contacts and data protection

10. **Unconfirmed sign-ups you have since worked with** (added details,
    tagged, listed, sent an offer) are no longer deleted after 30 days.
    The Privacy Notice, ROPA and retention schedule now say "unless we have
    since dealt with you". *Recommended: accept the wording.*
11. **Does in-house AML information count as a legal hold?** The studio's
    own KYC status and the AML fields in a contact's custom fields do not
    count today, so such a contact can be deleted. This belongs with October
    item 8. *Recommended: count them.*
12. **A corrected email after a bounce.** Offers reach the new address, but
    "Do not mail" (set by the bounce) stays on for newsletters until someone
    clears it. This belongs with October item 9. *Recommended: clear it
    automatically when the address changes.*
13. **Imported contacts with an honorific** (Dr, Prof, Sir…) but no
    salutation column are greeted "Dear <first name>". *Recommended: keep
    that.*
14. **How long files of deleted records are kept:** 90 days today. Raising
    it to about 400 days keeps every monthly backup fully restorable, but
    keeps removed photographs and the invoices of purged works longer.
    *Recommended: 90 days.*
15. **Files orphaned before this release.** A one-off sweep would remove
    files that no record points at. *Recommended: yes, after looking at its
    dry-run report (`/api/cron/storage-sweep?dry=1`).*
16. **Old data-subject requests with a late due date.** *Recommended: yes,
    correct the open ones with a one-off update.*

### Capture

17. **Makers from capture.** Capture no longer creates makers; it links an
    exact match or notes the name. *Recommended: keep this.*
18. **How long capture photos and invoice pages are kept after a push.**
    Today they are kept for ever. *Recommended: 8 weeks*, which needs a
    small database change.
19. **Purchase date of captures made abroad.** It is the London day. A
    New York evening sale can be a day off. *Recommended: leave it*; the
    date can be corrected in the studio, and an invoice's date replaces it.
20. **Capture contact undo window: 48 hours.** *Recommended: keep it.*

### Website

21. **Show "email opened" on offers?** Opens are now kept only in the
    admin-only email log, not counted as views. *Recommended: no.*
22. **Booking on Sundays.** The form greys Sundays out, but the server
    accepts them (for fairs). *Recommended: keep accepting them.*
23. **Sanity checks.** Three short queries are listed in the web fix's
    notes. They check for events with backwards dates, retired content types
    and a duplicate site-settings document. *Recommended: run them once in
    Sanity Vision.*

### Images, sync and imports

24. **A work whose photo will not upload to the website** for 6 hours is
    published without that photo. The alternative is to keep it off the
    website until someone looks. *Recommended: keep publishing.*
25. **FileMaker codes.** Which location does "SJ" mean (the seeded code is
    ST-JAMES)? Which categories are "A" and "C"? Once you answer,
    `pnpm migrate backfill-codes --apply` fills the works already imported.
    It does a dry run first.
26. **Japanese in the certificate's serif text** prints in a sans font.
    Adding a Japanese serif font costs about 8 MB per PDF function.
    *Recommended: leave it.*

## Part 4: what has to be done on the server, in order

The studio, website and capture code is deployed by Claude from `main` and
works before any of these steps, exactly as today. Each step adds what it
names.

1. **The October security session** (`docs/ops/security-runbook.md` steps
   1–5 and 5.4). It is still open, and the sign-in limits (F1) are critical.
   Then apply database updates 0089–0096:
   `scripts/apply-migrations.sh 0089 0096`.
2. **This round's database updates** 0097–0100, after 0089–0096, through the
   same tunnel: `scripts/apply-migrations.sh 0097 0100`. Run `pnpm test:db`
   first. They hold the review's database fixes, the sold-work guard, the
   image worker's retry lease and the search outbox. Each ends with the
   schema reload.
3. **Update the image worker** (`security-runbook.md` step 10, rewritten).
   Apply 0099 first.
4. **Start OpenSearch** (`docs/ops/opensearch-runbook.md`). It covers:
   - checking free memory and disk;
   - building the slim image;
   - adding the Caddy site;
   - setting the two passwords in Vercel (studio project, Production only);
   - finishing with Admin → Search → "Reconcile now".
5. **After deploying:** press Admin → Resync once. Some objects whose
   website square changed with the new flat-work rule get their new tile.
6. **Hosted Sanity Studio:** from `apps/web`, run `pnpm exec sanity deploy`.
   It picks up the schema changes (rich-text options, validation warnings,
   Create menu).
7. **Capture app:** check that the production deployment in Vercel is green.
   Then scan one business card, read one label and read one invoice, to
   check the AI calls.
8. **One-off clean-up on the server:** delete the old business-card photos
   in the `captures` bucket, folder `cards/`.

## Part 5: how this was checked

- Every finding was confirmed by a second reviewer before it was fixed.
  Every fix was reviewed again, with all checks rerun, and corrected where
  that review found a problem.
- Unit tests: [n] pass. OpenSearch integration tests ran against a real
  OpenSearch 3.9.0.
- Database tests: all migrations (0001–0100) apply on a fresh database, and
  every database test passes.
- Typecheck and production build of all three apps and every package pass.
- `pnpm audit`: no known vulnerabilities.
- The form-field guard confirms no autosave field was renamed by accident.
- The Caddy search block was validated with the real Caddy, and its allowed
  and refused requests are pinned by a test. The slim OpenSearch image was
  built and started locally.

Not checked here, because it needs the live systems:
- the live server steps in Part 4;
- the real AI calls from capture;
- Sanity Studio after deploy;
- a few browser-only behaviours on the website: the full-screen view fitting
  the screen, Back restoring the archive position, and opening a work from
  search on the same page.

Please look at those once after the release.
